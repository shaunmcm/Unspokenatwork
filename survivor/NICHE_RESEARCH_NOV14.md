# Workplace Survivor: niche research + "no AI voice" rules (Nov 14, 2026)

Instagram itself can't be opened from this workspace, so this is built from public coverage of the pages (articles, analytics sites), not from scrolling their feeds.

## 10 pages/creators reaching our audience, and what they do
1. Corporate Millennial Anxiety (@corporate_millennial_memes): about 297.6K followers, about 123K average Reel views (Qoruz, Jul 2026). Short, fictional, relatable work memes. The founder hit 10K followers in 2 weeks.
2. Work Memes Official: self-deprecating memes about "the pain and struggle of a 9-to-5 job."
3. Memes Off The Clock: about 25K followers. "Corporate memes for your lunch break dreams." Light, fast, shareable.
4. Kier Gaines (@kiergaines): about 1.6M followers. Licensed therapist and dad. Mixes comedy and serious posts, aims to be "100% authentic." His numbered lists ("things I learned after becoming a dad") went viral.
5. Man Therapy / Dr. Rich Mahogany: built for working-age men 25-54. Uses humor and straight talk ("part football coach, part drinking buddy, part therapist"). Drives men to a free 18-point "head inspection" quiz. A CDC-funded study found it works.
6. Dr. Brady Smith (@drbradysmith): a far-too-relatable viral post about men and dentist appointments. Everyday guy moments.
7. Alessandro Frosali: about 1M followers across platforms. Blunt "step up" talk to men with a strong personal style.
8. Taylor Calmus (Dude Dad) and Rob Kenney (Dad, How Do I?): dad creators. Warm, funny, everyday, practical.
9. Justin Birckbichler: heavy men's health topics, uses humor to ease the discomfort.
10. The Exit Strategy (a Gumroad creator selling a quit-your-9-5 guide): blunt and direct ("STOP wasting time if you're miserable working your 9-5").
Trades/blue-collar groups (TIACS, Movember, construction campaigns): no fluff, respect the man's time, use the words he uses.

## Principles to borrow (without copying)
- A way in through humor: Man Therapy and the meme pages get men to look at hard stuff by making them laugh first. Mix in dry, self-aware humor about the job. Not every post should be heavy.
- One hyper-specific shared moment beats a speech: the parking-lot minute, the 6:58 text from the boss, the "quick question" at 4:59, "we're a family here."
- Real voice, mixed tone (Kier Gaines): some posts funny, some serious, all sounding like one real guy.
- Numbered "things I learned" lists are a proven format.
- Straight talk in men's language: gruff and direct, not a therapist voice. Frame getting help as practical: "treat it like a busted knee."
- Quiz as the funnel (Man Therapy's "head inspection" is a free quiz): call ours a quick "exit check" or "5-minute exit quiz."
- Built to be sent: give him a reason to send it to his buddy or slip it to his wife.

## Remove anything that sounds like AI (banned from on-screen text and captions)
- Em dashes (—). Use periods or commas.
- "It's not X. It's Y." / "That's not weakness. That's..." / "Not because X. Because Y." Researchers call this the single most-flagged AI pattern.
- Stock cliffhangers: "Here's the part nobody tells you", "Hold that thought", "Stay with me", "Let that sink in", "Here's the truth", "Read that again", "And that changes everything."
- Rule-of-three fragment drumrolls: "Same shift. Same boss. Same pressure."
- Tidy slogan endings on every post ("He didn't quit first. He built first."). Use the slogan at most once a week.
- Words: delve, navigate, journey, unlock, empower, game-changer, testament, landscape, crucial, embrace, thrive, resonate, elevate, seamless, transformative, realm, tapestry.
- Self-applause: "This is powerful", "This changes everything."
- Every paragraph the same length; an emoji arrow on every first line.

## How to sound human instead
- Write like a guy texting a buddy: contractions, plain words, the odd fragment or run-on, a bit of dry humor.
- One concrete, specific detail per caption (a time, a place, a thing the boss says).
- Vary the shape: some captions 4 lines, some 12. Not always "hook, pain, figure, step, CTA."
- Founder stories are optional (about 1 post in 3), told in one or two plain sentences, never as a lecture.
- CTA in plain words: 'Comment "EXIT" and I'll send you the quiz.'
