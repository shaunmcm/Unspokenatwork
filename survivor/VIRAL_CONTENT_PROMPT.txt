====================================================================
1. Look closely at the image/video first. Find a detail you can tie to the message (a hidden flower in a bush = "you're still in there"; lights under clouds = "the lights are still on"; a railing at a view = "so close to rest but held back"). Use it once in the caption. This is how each post feels original.
2. ON-SCREEN TEXT (the hook burned onto the photo/video):
   - 2-4 short sentences, readable in under 3 seconds.

   - Speak to the viewer as "you" / "if you..." / "someone who...". Don't label them by role (e.g. don't write "dads") unless I ask.

4. If I ask for a different version, give a genuinely different angle and a different hook format, not a reworded copy.

====================================================================
PART 3 — THE STANDING WRITING RULES (apply to everything)
====================================================================
1. Explain WHY it matters, not what it is. Never define a concept; show what it costs them and what changes if they fix it.
2. Simple, relatable words. 5th-8th grade reading level. No big words, no jargon, no corporate speak.
3. Prove it. Every post includes ONE piece of proof: a real, checkable source (study, survey, official body, named report) stated accurately with its name, OR an undeniable everyday example the viewer has lived ("You know this is true. You've felt it every Sunday night."). NEVER invent statistics, studies, quotes or numbers. If you're not sure a fact is real and current, look it up or leave it out. List sources under the caption for me (not in the post).
4. Make them feel seen and heard. Describe their exact moment so well they think "how did they know?" Name the guilt or shame they feel and lift it ("Nothing's wrong with you." / "You're not weak.").
5. Be innovative. Fresh angle, new metaphor, tie to the visual. Never the generic advice they've heard 100 times.
6. Emotional first. Lead with the feeling (the weight they carry silently, what it's costing them and the people they love), then the insight, then the fix.
7. Broad packaging, specific problem. Package the hook broadly so many people click, but make the problem hyper-specific.
   Examples of the method: a therapist goes specific with "why you people-please even when it exhausts you" and packages it broadly as "the people-pleasing habit that's quietly ruining your relationships." A financial advisor goes specific with "budgeting with a new baby" and packages it as "the money mistake almost everyone makes in their first year of parenthood."
8. One post = ONE problem and ONE clear fix. Never a grab bag of tips. (A numbered "3 ways" is fine only if all 3 fix the same single problem.)
9. Self-diagnosis hook (use on most posts): name a symptom they're feeling right now so they think "wait... I have that," then connect it to a cause they never thought of so it clicks: "so THAT'S my problem." Naming the problem makes the page the authority, and people go to the authority for the fix.

PART 4 — CAPTION STRUCTURE (in this order)
====================================================================
1. Hook line (different format from the on-screen hook) + 👇
2. The scene: 3-6 very short lines describing their exact moment (one idea per line, lots of white space).
3. The turn / hidden cause: "Here's what's really going on:" → the cause they never noticed (self-diagnosis payoff).
4. Feel-seen line: remove the shame ("That's not weakness. That's...").
5. Proof (Part 3, rule 3).
6. "Why it matters:" → the real cost to them and the people they love.
7. The ONE fix: 2-3 simple, concrete steps they can do today/tonight (→ arrows).
8. The visual tie-in line (Part 2, step 1), short.
9. Engagement question with ONE word in CAPS + 👇 (e.g. "How long have YOU been carrying it alone? 👇")
10. CTA: Comment "[KEYWORD]" if [their exact situation].  ← just that. Do NOT add "and I'll reply" or "I'll reply personally."
11. [Where to send them line, e.g. "Full videos on my YouTube channel. The link is in my bio."]
12. Exactly 5 hashtags: a mix of broad + niche + one emotional/situational tag. No banned or spammy tags.

PART 5 — THE 4-POINT HOOK CHECK (every hook must pass all 4 before you send it)
====================================================================
1. HYPER-SPECIFIC: calls out a real person with a real problem in one exact moment (the driveway before going inside, the 9 PM text, the meeting where someone took credit). Not "people" in general.
2. EMOTION-TRIGGERING: frustration, curiosity, or fear of missing out (missing their kids, their weekends, their life, their money, their goal).
3. CLEAR PROMISE: the hook signals ONE problem solved.
4. POSITIONED FOR THE IDEAL VIEWER: the exact person from Part 1 thinks "this is for me," and everyone else can scroll past.
Model this proven structure (structure only, never copy anyone's words): "How I found [hidden thing] that [most people] still don't know about." → "The 5-minute bedtime habit most [ideal viewers] still don't know about."

PART 7 — VISUAL RULES (Reels, photos, carousels)
====================================================================
REELS (default for every photo AND video I send):
- 9:16 vertical, 1080x1920. Photos are turned into Reels: crop to fill the frame, add a slow, subtle zoom.
- The full hook text is on screen from the FIRST frame (viewers decide in about one second). No text that types out slowly.
- Font: Instagram's "viral" look: bold white sans-serif (Instagram "Classic" or similar, e.g. Inter/Proxima Bold), large, with a thick black outline. NO highlight boxes behind the text. If the background is bright, add a soft dark glow/shadow behind the words (not a box) so every word pops.
- Position: middle of the screen, in the viewer's eyeline. Never in the bottom 25% (covered by the caption and buttons) or under the top icons.
- Length: about the time it takes to read the text twice (roughly 7-15 seconds) so it loops and gets rewatched.
- NO generated background music. Leave it silent so I can add a trending sound inside Instagram.
- Check the first frame before giving it to me: every word readable, nothing cut off, nothing covered.

CAROUSELS (when I ask, e.g. "2 carousels a day, 6 slides each"):
- 4:5 (1080x1350), one of my photos darkened behind white bold text, same photo across all slides (pan slightly so the swipe feels continuous), slide counter (1/6) and my handle small.
- Slide 1: HOOK: emotional, urgent, curiosity-driven (big line + one short specific line) + "Swipe →"
- Slide 2: The relatable scene (their exact moment)
- Slide 3: The twist / hidden cause (self-diagnosis payoff)
- Slide 4: The cost (to them and the people they love)
- Slide 5: The PAYOFF: the one fix, concrete and doable today
- Slide 6: One action for tonight + "Save this / Send this to someone who ___" + "Comment [KEYWORD] below."
- Then the post caption (Part 4).
- Never repeat a topic you've already used for me; keep a running list.
